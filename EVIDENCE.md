# Where the coach's rules come from

Every rule the code applies, what it is based on, and how strong that basis is. Checked 2026-11-26.

**Strength:**
- **Research:** controlled studies or meta-analyses.
- **Some research:** a few studies, small, or contested.
- **Practice:** what experienced coaches use, with little formal testing.
- **Your data:** set from this athlete's own history.

**Verdict:**
- ✅ matches expert practice.
- ⚠️ matches with a caveat.
- ❌ departs from it (see "Open findings" at the end).

## Load and fitness

| Rule in the code | Basis | Strength | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fitness / fatigue / form = 42-day / 7-day load averages (CTL, ATL, TSB), read from Intervals.icu | Banister impulse-response model (1975); Coggan's Performance Manager | Research (model), practice (thresholds) | ✅ |
| ~7×CTL a week holds fitness; normal ramp +2 (Base) / +3 (Build) CTL a week | Follows from the model; Friel / TrainingPeaks suggest ~3-8 CTL/week for trained athletes | Practice | ✅ |
| Comeback ramp +5 CTL/week while CTL < 50, capped at your 2022-24 top weeks | Fitness you've had before returns faster than new fitness is built (retained structural adaptations; "muscle memory" in training studies); cap from your own history | Some research + your data | ⚠️ aggressive by the standard measures (fatigue ≈ 1.6× fitness), chosen on purpose; see finding 1 |
| Recovery week after 2-4 loading weeks, at 50-70% of holding load | The classic 3:1 / 2:1 loading pattern; Issurin, Bompa | Practice | ✅ |
| Base before Build; Build unlocks after 6 good Base weeks or when HR drift < 5% on long rides | Friel's base-building and aerobic decoupling test | Practice | ✅ |
| Taper: the week before an A race at 70-85% of holding load, intensity kept | Bosquet 2007 meta-analysis: cut volume 41-60%, keep intensity and frequency, over 8-14 days | Research | ⚠️ the cut is about right, but only one week; the research favours ~2 |
| Weekly target missed: keep key sessions, drop filler, never cram | Coaching consensus: consistency over catching up | Practice | ✅ |

## Daily readiness

| Rule in the code | Basis | Strength | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| HRV: 7-day average vs your baseline ± ½ SD | Kiviniemi 2007; Plews 2012-13; Vesterinen 2016 (HRV-guided endurance training) | Research | ✅ |
| Adjust training from HRV and feel rather than a fixed calendar | Javaloyes 2019 / 2020 (cyclists): HRV-guided at least as good as predefined, gains slightly larger, small groups | Some research | ✅ |
| Form (TSB) lines relative to the plan's ramp: amber 10 below the form the ramp builds, red 15 below, never above −20 / −30 | TrainingPeaks / Friel (−10 to −30 is the "productive" range); planned vs unexpected fatigue; HRV-guided research | Practice + some research | ✅ since 2026-11-26 (finding 1) |
| Training monotony > 2.0 counts only with the last 7 days' load above the plan's weekly maximum | Foster 1998: illness was linked to high monotony **together with** high weekly strain | Some research | ✅ since 2026-11-26 (finding 2) |
| ACWR (7-day vs 28-day load) > 1.3 caution, > 1.5 high; wider during a comeback | Gabbett 2016; strongly criticised since (Impellizzeri 2020: statistical artefacts) | Contested | ⚠️ fine as a caution, never as a stop (which is how it's used) |
| Illness early warning: overnight HR, HRV, breathing rate vs your last 28 nights | Wearable studies (Radin 2020, Mishra 2020): these rise 1-3 days before symptoms | Research | ✅ |
| One poor night → keep the session, warm-up test; 2+ poor nights → recovery signal | Sleep-loss studies show one night barely changes endurance; several nights do | Some research | ✅ |
| Amber = same session ~20% shorter + warm-up test; red = rest or easy | Common coaching practice (adjust the dose, don't cancel) | Practice | ✅ |

## Findings

**Milestone, 2026-11-26: findings 1 and 2 approved and applied.** The athlete asked for a less
aggressive amber; from this day the system trains harder on planned fatigue. When asking later
why training feels harder, this is the change to look at first. The adjustment log has it, so the
quarterly review shows what followed (markers 4 weeks on).

**1. The amber form line fought the comeback plan (fixed 2026-11-26).** The plan ramps fitness +5 a week. With the
42/7-day model, a steady ramp of r a week holds form at about −5×r, so −25 here, by design. The
amber line at −20 therefore trims most days of the plan's own loading weeks, which works out to a
real ramp of about +4. Your data already shows it: form went from −8 to −18.5 in the first 4 days of
the ramp. Coaches treat *planned* fatigue differently from *unexpected* fatigue. The fix that matches
that, and the HRV-guided research, is to make the form line relative to the plan: amber when form is
more than ~10 below what the plan's ramp expects (so about −35 at +5, −25 at +3). Recovery signals
(HRV, sleep, resting HR, how you feel) keep deciding whether you are absorbing it. The −30 red stop
would move the same way, with a floor.

**2. Monotony alone made a day amber (fixed 2026-11-26).** Foster's finding was about monotony **combined with** high
weekly strain. With daily training and few full rest days, monotony is above 2.0 most weeks, so on its
own it says little. It was the only amber reason on 2026-11-26 at 13:00. Closer to the research: count
it only together with high strain (your top weeks) or with a recovery signal.

**3. The taper is one week (open).** The research favours 8-14 days with the same volume cut. That matters
only once an A race is on the calendar.
